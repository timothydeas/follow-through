# FollowThru — Decision Compass

*The one place to check before any product, UX, copy, or scope decision. If a choice doesn't sit right against this, stop and rethink it.*

---

## 1. Where we're headed (business direction)
- **Mission:** help people follow through *in the moment* — the gap between setting a goal and acting on it.
- **Differentiator / moat:** privacy-first, local-only (no accounts, no cloud, no analytics, no ads). Trust and psychological safety *are* the product, not a feature bolted on.
- **Near-term outcome (first Play launch):** earn genuine word-of-mouth by actually helping. Quality and retention through real value — never growth hacks or nagging.
- **North star + metrics:** the specific north-star metric, leading indicators, and guardrails live in the **lean PRD**. *(Paste the exact ones here so this doc stands alone.)*
- **Shaping constraint:** no analytics. We can't A/B test or measure funnels — so decisions lean on the science below plus qualitative beta feedback, and we bias toward reversible, low-regret bets.

## 2. What users told us
- **42.9% of beta testers flagged the reflection questions as the top friction.** One didn't know "where answering was headed."
- **The rule it became:** minimize the answering burden. Embody the science; don't interrogate the user. Depth is optional — only the goal name is required; everything else is skippable.

## 3. The behavioral science we're built on
- **Implementation intentions (Gollwitzer, d ≈ .65) — the core lever.** Pre-deciding "when [cue], I will [action]" is the strongest behavior-change move we have. It's the cue-based plan, set at goal creation.
- **Present bias (Milkman; O'Donoghue & Rabin) — why people slip.** In the moment we over-value the immediate and easy, so the future goal loses. Implementation intentions beat it by making the decision *before* the vulnerable moment.
- **Optimally-abstract, aspirational goals (Fishbach).** Framed around meaning, not identity labels (anti-Atomic-Habits) and not chore-metrics.
- **Setbacks = information, not identity (Fishbach, Duckworth).** A miss is data about the plan, never a verdict on the person. Never diagnose the user.
- **Ostrich effect (Fishbach).** People dodge unpleasant feedback that would help. Surface it gently; reframe; never shame.
- **What-the-hell effect (Milkman; Sharif's emergency reserve).** Flex-protected streaks with planned grace, so one slip doesn't trigger "I've blown it" quitting. Fresh-start framing: *begin again.*
- **Grit (Duckworth).** *Persist through the unknown until it becomes the obvious.*

## 4. Motivation, cognition & psychology
- **Autonomy support / intrinsic motivation (SDT):** offer, never coerce. Optional depth, user choice, gentle tone.
- **Cognitive recollection:** the cue-based plan works because the cue *retrieves* the intended action from memory at the right moment — design so the right thing comes to mind when it counts.
- **Psychological safety = privacy:** local-only isn't just technical; it's what lets people be honest with themselves.
- **Voice:** warm, plain, non-judgmental. Tagline: *in every moment.*

## 5. How we decide (run every choice through these)
- **JTBD first:** what is the user hiring this for?
- **Does it move the north star?** If not, why are we doing it?
- **Out-of-scope is as important as in-scope.** Cut ruthlessly.
- **Minimum to test the hypothesis** — not the full product.
- **Iterate on the core problem before adding features.**
- **Does it add answering/interaction burden?** (Remember the 42.9%.) If yes — can it be optional?
- **Is it grounded in the science above, or is it just a feature?**
- **Does it respect autonomy and privacy?**
- **Is it reversible / low-regret?** (We can't measure, so prefer bets we can undo.)
