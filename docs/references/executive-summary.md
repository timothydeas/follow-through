# Executive Summary — FollowThru: Reminders-Through-Association MVP Release

**Prepared:** June 11, 2026 · **Ship:** readiness-gated → production submission (prior 14-day closed beta satisfies Play's closed-test requirement; identical survey re-run at staged rollout) · **Platform:** Native Android (phone + tablet)

## The opportunity

People fail to follow through on their intentions not because they lack willpower, but because they forget at the precise moment they could act. Rogers and Milkman (2016) demonstrated that tying an intention to a single distinctive cue — encountered at the actionable moment — raised follow-through from 42% to 74% in the lab, outperformed written reminders in cluttered environments (92% vs. 78%), and lifted real-world coupon redemption 36% in a field test. Critically, their work also showed people systematically undervalue this technique, which means a product that operationalizes it is doing something users would not do for themselves. This release of FollowThru — an existing, live Android app (`com.ideasinc.followthrough`) — operationalizes it: a privacy-first experience where a user walks one clear spine — goal → relevant self-knowledge → implementation intention → one distinctive cue → reminder surfaced at the moment — entirely by hand, entirely on-device.

## What we learned from FollowThru, and what changes

FollowThru's closed beta (n=7, directional) was unambiguous about why a scientifically sound mechanism failed to land. Forty-three percent of testers couldn't tell where to start or what the app was for; the same share found the reflection questions burdensome and the accessibility lacking (TalkBack, contrast, text size); 29% hit raw usability failures — text fields they couldn't see while typing, keyboards covering content, a red box overlapping the question copy. Testers also flagged light/dark theme mismatch with the store listing, splash messages that auto-dismissed before they could be read, broken landscape orientation, no way to undo an accidental completion, and a desire for goal-centric progress. The encouraging signal in the same data: testers who got past the confusion said the app was "helpful for thinking about my goals," and one non-journaler was converted by the positioning line itself — "we all make goals; following through is the hard part."

This release treats every one of these as a launch requirement, not a backlog item: purpose-first onboarding with a 30-second worked example; a lead-light check-in (one tap — Done, Snooze, Not today — with reflection optional and collapsed); inputs that are visibly typeable and never occluded by the keyboard; WCAG 2.1 AA across both themes with verified light/dark parity; user-controlled (never timed) dismissal; portrait and landscape support; and undoable actions everywhere. Store positioning leads with the beta-validated hook — "Setting goals is easy. Following through is the hard part." — with the forgetting thesis carried gently in the supporting line ("FollowThru helps you remember at the moment it matters — one vivid cue at a time"), deliberately avoiding willpower-confrontational framing.

## How the MVP is grounded in research

The brief's one-cue principle comes directly from the distinctiveness findings: a cue works because it stands out from what surrounds it and what preceded it, so the app enforces exactly one user-chosen cue per intention, always paired with full intention text so meaning never depends on a single sense or channel. Calboli (2025) supplies the ethical architecture: because the user authors the cue, the intention, and the moment, both dimensions of autonomy are preserved — self-agency (the reason behind the preference is the user's own) and autocracy (the reminder is effortlessly resistible, with "not today" one tap away and no punitive streak mechanics). The progress mechanic follows the streak science precisely: Milkman's work shows streaks motivate but rigidity backfires (the what-the-hell effect), Sharif & Shu's emergency-reserves studies show a limited two-pass allowance beats both rigid and fully-slack designs, and the fresh-start effect (Dai, Milkman & Riis) dictates that a failed week points forward to Monday rather than backward at the miss. MindShift (CHI 2024) validates the longer arc — context-aware, goals-anchored persuasion measurably outperforms static reminders (acceptance up 22.5%, usage down ~10%, addiction-scale scores down 34.7%) — while also documenting exactly why we are not shipping AI now: templated-feeling generated content, hallucination risk, and the privacy exposure of routing personal context through cloud LLMs. Market benchmarks frame the bar: Android Day-7 retention averages roughly 5% (Pushwoosh 2025 benchmark study) against a ~10.7% global cross-platform average, and apps that deliver first value within about three minutes see roughly double the retention — which is why first-session reminder creation in under three minutes is our activation gate.

## MVP scope in one paragraph

Three features, nothing else. The Guided Reminder Builder walks the spine in four steps with starter templates to defeat the blank slate. The Self-Knowledge Palette captures passions, interests, and learnings at the person level and barriers and progress at the goal level — lightweight chips, never a quiz — resolving the brief's open question with a goal-anchored path that draws from a reusable personal palette. Cue-Fire Delivery surfaces the one cue plus the full intention text at the scheduled moment with one-tap, undoable, judgment-free responses. Progress is a flexible weekly streak — the streak survives any week with at most two missed reminders via auto-applied emergency passes — paired with a never-resetting lifetime follow-through count and fresh-start copy when a week fails; rigid break-the-chain streaks are explicitly excluded. Out of scope: all AI/ML, sub-goal hierarchies and achievement showcases, accounts, cloud sync, and social features. The build is native Kotlin/Compose with local-only persistence and no backend, prototyped first in Loveable as the Android Studio reference.

## How we'll know it worked

Because the app is privacy-first, measurement is consent-tiered rather than absolutist — the hard line is that nothing leaves the device automatically, but "no automatic tracking" is not the same as "no measurement." Four tiers, all free at launch: lagging outcomes from Play Console aggregates (platform-anonymous), leading indicators held in on-device counters, proxy metrics standing in for what we choose not to observe directly, and counter metrics guarding against winning the number while losing the user. A voluntary "share my stats" path (payload-visible, user-initiated, per-send) is a planned fast-follow; see measurement-options.md for the full free/paid × local-only/shared matrix and the revisit triggers. This corrects an earlier absolutist "no transmission ever" framing inherited from the brief: the brand promise "no tracking" stays literally true under consent-tiered measurement, while leaving a free path to real funnel numbers.

**Lagging indicators (North Star and downstream outcomes).** The North Star is **Day-7 device retention ≥12%** via Play Console aggregates, against the ~5% Android average (Pushwoosh 2025) and ~10.7% global average — this is the direct readout of the stated business outcome: acquire users and retain them through the first seven days. Downstream lagging outcomes on the same instrument: Day-30 retention trend and sustained engagement over the six-month window in which the local self-knowledge corpus accumulates for the future AI iteration, plus store-rating trajectory as the lagging voice of user satisfaction.

**Leading indicators (predict the North Star, on-device counters only).** (1) **First-session activation:** ≥60% of new users create their first cue-attached reminder in under three minutes — grounded in the finding that apps delivering first value within ~3 minutes see roughly double the retention, and aimed squarely at the beta's 43% "where to start" failure. (2) **Follow-through rate:** % of delivered reminders marked Done within 24h — the live test of the Rogers & Milkman hypothesis (74% vs. 42% with distinctive cues). (3) **Palette-drawn cue share:** % of reminders whose cue came from the user's own passions, interests, or learnings — the direct signal for the stated business outcome that users find value entering their self-knowledge.

**Proxy metrics (stand-ins forced by the privacy-first design — distinct from leading indicators).** Because we refuse server-side behavioral analytics, several things we care about most are measured by proxy: the **identical beta survey re-run** at staged rollout proxies comprehension and UX quality (the before→after deltas on "where to start" 43%, "reflection questions" 43%, "accessibility" 43%, "look/layout" 29%); the **palette-cue vs. generic-cue follow-through delta** in local counters proxies the causal hypothesis in the absence of a randomized trial; Play Console aggregates themselves proxy cohort behavior we never observe per-user; and **palette depth per user** (items entered) proxies readiness of the local data corpus the AI iteration will depend on.

**Counter metrics (guardrails).** 24-hour uninstall rate down versus the FollowThru baseline; store rating ≥4.0; crash-free sessions ≥99%; and notification opt-out / permanent-snooze ≤15% — the autonomy check, per Calboli, that the cue is reminding rather than nagging. If a leading indicator improves while a counter metric degrades, the counter metric wins the argument.

At staged rollout we re-run the identical survey and fill the before→after column, closing the loop the FollowThru scorecard defined.

## The bridge to the AI iteration

Every element of this MVP is also infrastructure for the next six months. The palette, barriers, learnings, and the event log of which cues fired and which were honored constitute exactly the local, user-authored corpus the future persuasive-AI iteration needs to suggest and assemble distinctive cues in near-real-time (within ~2 minutes of an actionable moment). MindShift proves that goals-and-context-aware generated persuasion outperforms static content; Calboli's framework defines the guardrails it must ship with — personalization that strengthens the user's ability to act on their own preferences rather than steering them, with every AI suggestion editable, declinable, and explained. Because the MVP runs human-authored, it doubles as the control condition: when AI-assisted cue creation arrives, we will already have the baseline follow-through rate it must beat. The business sequence is therefore clean — prove value and retention now with the manual spine, accumulate consented local data through genuine daily use, then layer intelligence onto a mechanism users already trust.
